## ⚠️ Time-Sensitive: Shunga Exhibition — Closes May 31

"A Contest of Allure: Katsushika Hokusai and Keisai Eisen" — the third edition of an ongoing series showing ~100 shunga (Japanese erotic woodblock prints) including Hokusai's famous "Tako to Ama" (The Dream of the Fisherman's Wife — the octopus one).

- **Location:** Shinjuku Kabukicho Noh Stage, Lions Plaza Shinjuku 2F, Kabukicho 2-9-18. Right in the heart of Kabukicho.
- **Hours:** 11am–7pm daily; Fri/Sat until 9pm
- **Cost:** ¥2,200 (¥1,500 students). Tickets via GetYourGuide.
- **Age:** 18+ only, ID required
- The Kabukicho location makes this easy to combine with a walk through the entertainment district

## Muscle Girls Bar

Staffed by competitive female bodybuilders in neon activewear. You order cocktails or protein shakes and can challenge them to arm wrestling, request poses, take photos, or get slapped (yes, literally).

- **Location:** Ikebukuro (Toshima-ku), near Ikebukuro Station
- **Cost:** ~¥6,000/person. "Muscle Dollar" currency — ¥1,000 = 10 Muscle Dollars, spent on activities (pose photo, slap, being lifted). All-you-can-drink at ¥5,000+ tier.
- **Session format:** 80–90 min timed sessions
- **Reservation:** Required — weekends sell out. Book in advance online.
- Very foreigner-friendly, used to international groups

## Meguro Parasitological Museum

The world's only parasite museum. Founded 1953. Two floors of ~300 specimens in formaldehyde jars including an 8.8-meter (29 ft) tapeworm extracted from a human host. First floor: parasites in fish and animals. Second floor: human parasites — liver flukes, roundworms, all of it.

- **Location:** 4-1-1 Shimomeguro, Meguro-ku. 10-min walk from Meguro Station.
- **Cost:** Free (donations welcome)
- **Hours:** 10am–5pm. Closed Mondays and Tuesdays.
- **Time needed:** 30–45 minutes
- Gift shop sells parasite-themed keychains and merch — excellent souvenirs
- Don't miss the 8.8m tapeworm

## Shibari / Rope Events

Accessible but requires active monitoring — you won't stumble in.

- **shibari.events/Tokyo** — the best current event listing. Filter for "Performance" or "Showcase."
- **Club nights:** Contact, DOMMUNE, and art-focused venues host shibari performance nights. Check iflyer.tv for current listings.
- **Onawa Asobi Festival:** Annual 2-day kinbaku event running since 2009. Check onawa-europe.com for 2026 Tokyo dates.
- **Hajime Kinoko's Ichinawakai school:** The most reputable name in contemporary shibari art, treats it as aesthetics rather than kink. Events tracked via his social channels.
- Workshops: ¥3,000–¥8,000. Club performance nights: standard club entry.
- Monitor starting ~May 18 — events for your window get announced close to date.

## Underground Art / Experimental Galleries

**NANZUKA UNDERGROUND** — The definitive Tokyo gallery bridging street culture, illustration, design, and fine art. Shows underappreciated post-war Japanese artists (Keiichi Tanaami, Toshio Saeki, Harumi Yamaguchi). Comparable to what Deitch Projects was for NYC.
- Primary: Harajuku. Also at Shibuya PARCO (2G space — gallery + art toy shop).
- Free. Typically Tue–Sat 11am–7pm.

**SCAI The Bathhouse** — International-caliber contemporary art inside a 200-year-old converted public bathhouse. High ceilings, original wooden architecture. Worth visiting even if the show isn't compelling.
- Yanaka, 8-min walk from Nippori Station. Free. Tue–Sat 12pm–6pm.
- Combine with the Yanaka neighborhood (old Tokyo feel, vintage shops, record stores).

**Tokyo Arts and Space (TOKAS)** — Government-supported emerging artist space. Currently running TOKAS-Emerging 2026 (April–June). Free.

**Shimokitazawa live houses** — Where Japanese indie and underground music actually lives. 20+ small venues (Shelter: 250 cap; Garage: 120 cap raw; ERA; 440; CAVE). Shows ¥1,500–¥3,000, start 6–8pm. Completely authentic, almost no tourists.

## Clubs & Underground Music

**Contact** — Tokyo's most credible underground room. Concrete, industrial, no photography. Techno, house, experimental. The crowd is there for the music.
- Dogenzaka area, Shibuya basement. ~¥2,000–¥4,000.
- Hours: 11pm until dawn. Arrive midnight–1am.
- Bring passport (age verification, legal drinking is 20+ in Japan, strictly enforced).
- Check RA (ra.co) for specific lineups the week before.

**Womb** — Tokyo's landmark club since 1999. Four floors. Main floor has a Funktion-One sound system. Has hosted Carl Cox, Nina Kraviz, Ben Klock.
- Maruyama-cho, Shibuya. ¥3,000–¥5,000 door.
- Fri/Sat from ~11pm to 5am+. Advance tickets on RA for headliner nights.
- Saturday is the default recommendation.

**DOMMUNE** — Part club, part broadcast studio, part cultural archive. Capacity ~50 in-room; events livestreamed globally simultaneously. Founded by artist Naohiro Ukawa.
- Shibuya PARCO, B1F. Mon–Thu events, 19:00–midnight.
- Events confirmed only days in advance via @dommune Instagram. Start monitoring May 18.
- Genuinely underground without being velvet-rope. The Tokyo art/music intersection.

**Zero Tokyo** — Kabukicho Tower basement floors. Japan's largest night entertainment complex. More commercial/mainstream than Contact or Womb — but useful for a Kabukicho night out.

## Kabukicho / Shinjuku Entertainment District

Completely safe to walk at night. Brightly lit, heavy foot traffic, police. The main drag (Ichibangai) leads into the neon core. The Godzilla head on Hotel Gracery Shinjuku roars and lights up at intervals — visible from the street.

**Tokyu Kabukicho Tower** (opened 2023) — 48-floor tower restructuring the area:
- B1–B4: Zepp Shinjuku (1,500-cap live venue) — check for shows during your dates
- 2F: Shinjuku Kabuki Hall (entertainment food hall)
- 3F: Namco TOKYO (arcade/character events)
- 9–10F: 109 Cinemas Premium Shinjuku

**Host clubs** — Men in tailcoats entertain female customers through conversation, games, drinks, and the "Champagne Call" spectacle. Not prostitution — it's performance/companionship.
- Tourist-accessible via **Smappa! Group** (smappa.net/global) — English-friendly, runs guided Kabukicho experiences. GetYourGuide also lists a 90-minute host club experience with English guide (~¥30,000/person).
- First-visit "shokai" plan: ~¥500–¥2,000 entry + drinks. Regular bottles go fast.
- **Warning:** Any venue that doesn't show prices upfront — leave immediately. Use a guide service for hostess bars; unguided = foreigners routinely get badly overcharged.

## Love Hotels

Completely accessible, no complication. Walk in, select room from a touchscreen (photos + prices), pay, go. Fully anonymous — no front desk interaction, no ID.

**Two modes:** "Rest" (2–3 hrs, any time of day) · "Stay" (overnight)
**Pricing:** Rest ¥6,500–¥10,000 · Stay ¥13,000–¥22,000 (higher weekends)

**Love Hotel Hill (Dogenzaka, Shibuya)** — 3-min walk from Shibuya Station. Highest concentration in Tokyo. Worth walking through for the exteriors alone.

Notable specific hotels:
- **Sweets Hotel (Ruby / Cube):** Candy-themed and chocolate-themed buildings side by side. Doughnut pillows, faux chocolate baths, carousels, open-air baths, 24-hr karaoke rooms.
- **Hotel OLDSWING:** Mid-century jazz café aesthetic. "A Jazz Café You Can Stay At."
- **Hotel Lotus Shibuya:** Neo-Japanesque, in-room sauna, karaoke theater room.
- **Hotel SK Plaza:** 83 rooms across Balinese, Japanese Modern, Moroccan, and British themes.

Worth booking for one night as an experience rather than just walking past.

## Anime / Manga / Gaming

**Akihabara — Radio Kaikan** (yellow building next to station, 10 floors):
- 2F (Astop): 700+ display cases, rare figures, retro Famicom/Game Boy
- 4F (AmiAmi): Physical store of the major online retailer
- 5F (Kaiyodo): Ultra-detailed figures, Revoltech series
- 7F (JUNGLE): Vintage Showa-era toys, rare collectibles, tokusatsu

**Mandarake Akihabara** — 8 floors by theme. Largest in Japan. Used manga, figures, games, doujinshi.

**Super Potato** — Retro gaming, multiple floors. Famicom, Super Famicom, Saturn, dense stock.

**Nakano Broadway** — Better than Akihabara for otaku culture, less touristy, cheaper prices. Everything in one building. 5 min from Shinjuku on Chuo Line.
- Floors 3+ = Mandarake shops, each category-specialized (30+ individual stores within the building)
- Basement = vintage vinyl, old magazines, retro collectibles
- Ground floor: Get the oversized soft serve at Daily Chico — a legitimate Tokyo landmark

## Maid Cafes

**Maidreamin** — Most foreigner-friendly. Staff trained for international guests. Full experience: "moe moe kyun" ritual, omelette rice with ketchup art, group songs/dances. Multiple Akihabara locations.

**At-Home Cafe** — The original, opened 2004. English-speaking maids, more structured. Birthplace of "moe moe kyun."

**Cure Maid Cafe** — Opened 2001, the actual original maid cafe. More refined, long-skirt aesthetic, afternoon tea vibe. Less performatively cute.

Cost: Budget ¥1,500 minimum (entry + one drink). ¥3,000+ for full experience with food and Polaroid. Weekday afternoons = shortest waits.

## Other Weird Stuff

**Owl Cafe Akiba Fukurou** — 30+ owls, interact with them, former wedding photographer takes a free photo of each visitor.
- 3 min from Akihabara Station. ¥3,300/hr. Book in advance.

**Swallowtail Butler Cafe (Ikebukuro)** — The gold standard butler cafe. Men in tailcoats serving "milady" with full ceremony. No cameras, no breaking character. Genuinely committed atmosphere.
- ¥2,000–¥3,500 for a set. Reservation essential.

**Hedgehog Cafe — Petting Hedgehog Cheese** — Pick a hedgehog, hold it for 30 min. Also rabbits and owls. 3 min from Akihabara Station.
- ¥1,500/30 min + ¥1,000/additional 30 min. Unlimited soft drinks included.
