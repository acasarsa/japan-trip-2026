## Tokyo (May 21–25)

**May 22 — Ebisu / Meguro area (already have Ponga Ebisu 7pm + Bar Trench 9pm)**

**Sake Bar O** (Meguro) — Same neighborhood. 10-seat speakeasy, sommelier Kumi Hitomi, ~10 seasonal curated labels. No menu — she picks for you. Go 5–6pm before dinner, walk to Ponga Ebisu from there.

**EUREKA!** (Nishiazabu) — 10 min from Ebisu. 200+ varieties including cult labels like Aramasa that don't exist elsewhere. Only 12 counter seats — needs a reservation before the trip. If you want to find rare bottles, this is the place. Could go before or after Ponga Ebisu depending on reservation time.

**May 23 — Shibuya (shopping + baseball 6pm)**

**Sakestand Shibuya** (Dogenzaka, 2nd floor off a side street) — standing bar, craft sake in wine glasses with English info tags, casual and approachable. Good before or after baseball, or as an afternoon stop between shopping and the game.

**Sasagin** (Yoyogi-Uehara, 10 min from Shibuya) — Anglophone sommelier, 50+ rotating labels in glass fridges, strong namazake focus. One of the most consistently recommended in Tokyo. Could do this and Sakestand in the same evening — Sakestand as a warm-up, Sasagin for a proper sit.

**Akaoni** (Sangenjaya, 3 stops from Shibuya) — 100+ varieties, daily-changing food menu, hard-to-find seasonal bottles. A step further but still in the Shibuya orbit. Better on a night you don't have a specific end time.

**May 24 — Asakusa / Ueno / Sumo**

**Craft Sake Stand Yanaka** (Yanaka Ginza, adjacent to Ueno) — 80+ craft sake varieties, ¥550 flat per drink on a ticket system. Retro neighborhood. Afternoon format works well here — go after Senso-ji, before Sumo in the evening.

**May 25 — Heading to Hakone (Romance Car 7:37am from Shinjuku)**

**Hasegawa Saketen** (Tokyo Station GranSta) — tiny in-station bar brewing doburoku on premises. Not a destination, but if you're passing through Tokyo Station on the way back from somewhere the night of May 24, worth a stop. Low-key, no planning required.

**Skip for logistics:** Meishu Centre (original Hamamatsucho location closed 2020; surviving branch is in Ochanomizu — out of the way for your areas). Utsura Utsura (Shinsen, between Shibuya and Daikanyama) — worth knowing about for a slow evening but not a priority.

## Kyoto (May 26–29)

**May 27 — Fushimi Inari + Kinkaku-ji day**

The biggest sake opportunity of the trip is already baked into this day. Fushimi sake district is the next station south of Fushimi Inari on the Keihan Line — 5 min ride, same direction.

Natural sequence: Fushimi Inari 7am (early, before crowds) → walk or short train to Fushimi sake district by 10–11am → Fushimi tasting + lunch → Kinkaku-ji in the afternoon.

What to do in Fushimi:
- **Tsukinokatsura** (est. 1675, 4 brewers) — the most unusual stop. They made Japan's first sparkling nigori sake in 1964 and maintain 50-year-aged koshu. Small, not touristy. Start here.
- **Fushimi Sakagura Kouji (Sake Village)** — 18-brewery flight along a 23-meter counter. Opens noon. This is where you do the comparative tasting.
- **Gekkeikan Okura Sake Museum** — self-guided with good English, tasting of 3 varieties included. One of the oldest sake companies in the world (1637). No reservation needed.
- **Kizakura Brewery** — if anyone wants craft beer alongside sake, they have both on tap with a restaurant.

**May 27 or 28 evening — city center**

**Sake Bar Yoramu** (Nijo/Sanjo area) — The most singular sake bar in Kyoto. Israeli owner Yoram, 9 seats, aged sake spanning 30+ years, no menu. He interrogates you on flavor preferences and picks. Three-sake tasting set from ¥1,200. Open Wed–Sat 6pm–midnight — fits both May 27 (Wed) and May 28 (Thu). No reservations; call +81-75-213-1512 about 15 min before arriving. Groups max 3, so worth splitting the group if needed.

**Jizake Bar Zen** (Sanjo-Muromachi) — eccentric owner "Punch," strong aged sake focus, ~¥7,000 for 8 different sakes including long-aged varieties. Complementary to Yoramu if you want to do two bars in one night.

**Nihonshu Bar Asakura** (near Sanjo) — most English-friendly sake bar in Kyoto. Good fallback if Yoramu is full.

**Yamaneko Gion** — 50+ sake types + craft beer + natural wine. The crossover bar if the group has mixed interests. Gion nightlife district.

## Osaka (May 29–31)

**May 29 — Arrive from Kyoto (train 9–10am) + Osaka Castle + Sushi Hoshiyama 6pm**

**Asano Nihonshuten** (Umeda) — 150+ brands with a focus on local Osaka/Kansai sake. Hiru-nomi deal until 4pm: 3 sakes + snack for ¥700. Arrive in Osaka, drop bags, head to Asano before Osaka Castle. One of the best-value sake lineups in the city.

**May 30 — Kuromon + Dotonbori day**

**Shimada Shoten** (Kita-Horie) — looks like a liquor store; ask for a tasting and you're taken underground into a cellar the family has stocked over 70 years and 250+ brewery visits. Daily tasting of ~10 types at ¥220/glass. The most atmospheric sake experience in Osaka. Kita-Horie is a 10-min walk from Shinsaibashi.

**Sake Republic** (Shinsaibashi, 5th floor) — minimalist bar with city views, rare and limited bottles, ¥500–¥5,000/glass, owner recommends by preference. Walking distance from Dotonbori. Good evening stop after dinner.

**Hana Sake Bar** (Nishishinsaibashi) — English-speaking, sake flights, same general area. Call ahead to confirm still open.

**Half-day option, May 30:**

**Daimon Brewery** (Katano City, 30 min by Keihan Line from Namba) — English brewery tours by reservation, 1-hour or 2-hour with farm-to-table lunch pairing. Best craft sake brewery visit in the Osaka area. Requires booking before the trip. If you want a structured half-day brewery experience, this is it. Too far for a casual stop but worth the trip if intentional.
