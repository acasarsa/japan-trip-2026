## Tokyo

### Sake Bars

**Sasagin** — Yoyogi-Uehara (10 min from Shibuya)
The most consistently recommended sake bar in Tokyo for serious drinkers. An Anglophone sommelier runs it; English menus; ~50 rotating labels in glass fridges behind the counter. Strong namazake focus — May is peak namazake season. Food is izakaya-style (sashimi, tempura, ochazuke). Get a recommendation, don't just browse.
- Hours: Evenings; reservation recommended
- For you: Ask specifically for kimoto/yamahai options and what namazake is currently in

**Akaoni (Red Demon)** — Sangenjaya (3 stops from Shibuya on Den-en-toshi Line)
100+ varieties including hard-to-find bottles; rotating seasonal namazake; food menu changes daily (200+ dishes, intentionally sake-paired). One of the best bars for finding unusual labels.
- Hours: Mon–Fri 5:30pm–11:30pm · Sat 5pm–11:30pm · Sun 5pm–11pm
- Address: 2-15-3 Sangenjaya, Setagaya-ku

**EUREKA!** — Nishiazabu (Minato-ku, near Roppongi)
200+ varieties including cult labels like Aramasa (near-impossible to find elsewhere). Only 12 counter seats — 2-hour seatings, reservation essential. Run by sommelier Marie Chiba.
- Hours: Tue–Fri 6pm–11:30pm · Sat–Sun 3pm–11pm
- Address: 2F, 4-11-28 Nishiazabu, Minato-ku
- For you: The place to chase unicorn bottles (Aramasa No.6, rare limited releases). Worth booking in advance.

**Sake Bar O** — Meguro
Tokyo's sake speakeasy. Small square counter, hammered-tin interior, ~10 seasonal labels curated by sommelier Kumi Hitomi. Japanese-only communication is a potential barrier but the curation is singular. No menu — she picks for you based on preferences.
- Vibe: Dark, minimalist, high-concept
- Fits May 22 perfectly (same Meguro/Ebisu neighborhood as Ponga Ebisu and Bar Trench)

**Sakestand Shibuya** — Dogenzaka, Shibuya (2nd floor, look for the stairs)
Standing-only, hidden off a Shibuya side street. Rotating sake from across Japan in wine glasses with English info tags on each bottle. English-speaking staff. Very casual and approachable.
- Price: Reasonable; good for trying several glasses before dinner

**Utsura Utsura** — Shinsen (between Shibuya and Daikanyama)
Hot sake specialist — one of the few bars seriously focused on atsukan. 8-seat counter, 40 rotating labels from ¥500/glass or 180ml carafe. Seasonal otoshi (appetizer) included. Kimoto and yamahai labels reveal unexpected flavors when warmed.

**Craft Sake Stand Yanaka Junmai-ya** — Yanaka (Taito-ku, near Ueno/Asakusa)
80+ varieties of craft sake, ¥550 flat per drink. Ticket system: 8 tickets/¥4,000 or 10 tickets/¥4,950. Retro neighborhood on Yanaka Ginza shopping street. Good afternoon format if you're in the Asakusa area (May 24).

**Hasegawa Saketen** — Nihonbashi & Tokyo Station (GranSta)
Retail shop + bar hybrid. ~17 varieties by the glass from ¥400, including doburoku (cloudy unfiltered sake brewed on premises). The president is a legendary sake critic; staff are certified sommeliers. GranSta branch is inside Tokyo Station — convenient if you're passing through May 25 for the Romance Car.

**Japan Sake and Shochu Information Center** — Nishi-Shimbashi (Toranomon)
Industry association bar — part museum, part tasting. 100+ varieties on rotating 3–4 week cycles, from ¥100/cup. English staff. Free entry.
- Hours: Mon–Fri 10am–6pm (closed weekends)
- Good for orientation at the start of the trip

**Meishu Center** — Ochanomizu (note: the Hamamatsucho location closed 2020; this is the surviving branch)
150+ brands organized by prefecture. ¥200/cup; 3-sake sets get a discount. Swing jazz atmosphere, old-school snacks. Good for a long afternoon of regional comparison.
- Hours: Tue–Thu 12:30–20:30 · Fri–Sat 12:30–22:00 · Sun 12:30–19:30 (closed Mon)

### Labels to Seek in Tokyo

- **Aramasa** (Akita) — Cult natural-method brewery. Light, high-acid, wine-like. Near-impossible to find; best shot is EUREKA!
- **Tamagawa** (Kyoto/Tamba) — Bold and funky. Red Label (yamahai, muroka nama genshu) and White Label (natural yeast, unfiltered). Made by Philip Harper, the first non-Japanese toji in Japan.
- **Senkin** (Tochigi) — Former sommelier runs it. High acid, natural yeast, organic rice, Edo-era methods. Their "Nature" kimoto label is excellent.
- **Daishichi** (Fukushima) — Considered the master of kimoto. Rich, savory, complex. Widely available.
- **Terada Honke** (Chiba) — Japan's canonical natural sake producer. Gonin Musume is the flagship; Daigo no Shizuku uses bodaimoto. Very funky, acidic.
- **Gozenshu** (Okayama) — The brewery that revived bodaimoto. Their bodaimoto nigori is a must if you find it.
- **Juyondai** (Yamagata) — The unicorn. Near-mythical status, extremely limited. Order immediately if you see it on any menu.
- **Jikon** (Mie) — Cult status, deeply sought after. Light and elegant; unpasteurized versions especially prized.

## Kyoto

### Sake Bars

**Sake Bar Yoramu** — Nakagyo-ku (near Nijo/Sanjo, city center)
The most celebrated sake bar in Kyoto for serious drinkers. Israeli owner Yoram has been aging and collecting sake for 25+ years. 8–9 seats. Highlights: rare aged sake spanning 30+ years, wild-temperature-aged bottles with flavors you won't find elsewhere. No menu — Yoram interviews you and picks. Snacks include Japanese classics plus halloumi and falafel (his Israeli background). Vegetarian-friendly.
- Hours: Wed–Sat 6pm–midnight (closed Sun–Tue)
- Reservation: No reservations; call ~15 min before arrival at +81-75-213-1512 to hold a seat. Groups max 3.
- Price: 3-sake tasting set from ¥1,200
- Fits May 27 (Wed) or May 28 (Thu) evening perfectly

**Jizake Bar Zen** — Sanjo-Muromachi
Run by an eccentric character known as "Punch." Heavy emphasis on aged and unusual jizake (artisan/local sake). Large collection, cheap relative to Kyoto standards (~¥7,000 for ~8 different sakes including long-aged varieties). On the 5th floor of an older building.

**Nihonshu Bar Asakura** — City center (near Sanjo)
Most English-friendly sake bar in Kyoto. The owner speaks excellent English and guides foreign customers through the selection. Broad and varied collection including rare aged sake. Good for an explained sake education with no language barrier.

**Pontostand** — Sanjo/Kiyamachi
Small standing bar (under 20 people), English-speaking hosts Mako and Taku. Sake flights mixing Kyoto and regional labels, fruit-infused sake, plum wine. Good gyoza and snacks. Most social and casual of Kyoto's sake bars.

**Yamaneko Gion** — Gion (Higashiyama)
50+ types of sake alongside natural wines and tapped craft beer. A rare Kyoto bar bridging the craft beer / natural wine / sake worlds. Cozy and comfortable — fits anyone in the group who drinks craft beer or natural wine rather than sake.

### Fushimi Sake District

20 min south of central Kyoto (Chushojima Station on Keihan Line). Nearly 40 breweries in a walkable area along willow-lined canals. Already doing Fushimi Inari on May 27 — the sake district is the next station south. Natural sequence: Fushimi Inari at 7am → walk/train to sake district by late morning → tasting + lunch → continue to Kinkaku-ji.

**Fushimi Sakagura Koji (Sake Village)**
The anchor experience — 18-brewery comparative tasting along a 23-meter bar counter. 120 total brands available. One of the best sake flights in Japan.
- Opens midday

**Tsukinokatsura (Masuda Tokubee Shoten)**
The oldest surviving Fushimi brewery (founded 1675). Only 4 brewers. They produced Japan's first sparkling nigori sake in 1964; they also maintain possibly the oldest aged sake in Japan (50-year koshu). The "Junmai Nigori Sake Sparkling Nama" is the showpiece — light, bubbly, described as "rice champagne."
- Most unusual stop in Fushimi; not touristy

**Gekkeikan Okura Sake Museum**
One of Japan's oldest breweries (since 1637). Self-guided museum with English explanations. Tasting of 3 varieties included at the end. No reservation needed for small parties.

**Kizakura Brewery (Kizakura Kappa Country)**
Free museum covering sake and beer brewing. Restaurant serving sake + craft beer flights and kaiseki meals. Good for mixing sake and craft beer interests within the group.

### Labels Specific to Kyoto / Fushimi

- **Tsukinokatsura Sparkling Nigori Nama** — Buy at the brewery; hard to find outside Fushimi
- **Kaze no Mori (Yucho Shuzo, Nara)** — Available throughout Kyoto bars; naturally cloudy, bodaimoto-adjacent, wildly expressive. One of the best introductions to funky natural sake
- **Tamagawa** — Made in Tamba (Kyoto Prefecture); seek at craft-focused Kyoto bars

## Osaka

### Sake Bars

**Shimada Shoten** — Kita-Horie (Nishi-ku, near Shinsaibashi)
Looks like an ordinary liquor store. Ask for a tasting and you're taken through a hole in the floor into a dimly lit underground cellar stocked with bottles the family has assembled over 70 years and 250+ brewery visits. Daily tasting of ~10 types at ¥220/glass. Snacks at ¥210 each (cream cheese made from daiginjo, chunky miso, umeboshi). Founded 1954. The most atmospheric, genuinely off-the-tourist-path sake experience in Osaka.
- Price: From ¥220/glass — exceptional value

**Asano Nihonshuten** — Umeda (Kita-ku, near Osaka/Umeda stations)
150+ brands focused on local Osaka and Kansai breweries — rare to find these concentrated in one place. Specializes in junmai (no-additive sake). Hiru-nomi (afternoon drinking) deal until 4pm: 3 sakes + snack for ¥700. Best option if you want to explore local Kansai sake specifically. Arriving in Osaka morning of May 29? Go here before Osaka Castle.
- Hours: 11am–11pm
- Address: 1F Taiyuji Bldg, 2-17 Taiyuji-cho, Kita-ku

**Sake Republic** — Shinsaibashi (Chuo-ku, 5th floor)
Minimalist bar with city views, hidden on the 5th floor of an office building. Owner has unusual access to rare and limited-edition bottles; recommends by preference. Price range ¥500–¥5,000/glass. Sichuan-influenced food. Walking distance from Dotonbori.
- Address: 5F Ookini HOPETREE Shinsaibashi, 1-13-1 Nishishinsaibashi, Chuo-ku

**Hana Sake Bar** — Nishishinsaibashi (Chuo-ku)
English-speaking staff, sake flights, selection from small breweries. Good for the group member who wants guidance without a language barrier.
- Hours: Mon–Thu 6pm–11:30pm · Fri–Sat 6pm–1am (closed Sun)
- Note: Call ahead to confirm current status before visiting

### Sake Breweries (Osaka Area)

**Daimon Brewery** — Katano City (30 min from Namba via Keihan Line)
The most compelling brewery visit in the Osaka area for craft sake. English-language tours by reservation; 1-hour or 2-hour options. 2-hour includes farm-to-table lunch pairing at Mukune Restaurant (seasonal Japanese cuisine paired with Daimon's sake and fermented foods). Known especially for nigori sake.
- Location: 8 min walk from Kawachimori Station (Keihan Line)
- Reservation required in advance — book before the trip if interested

**Naniwa Shuzo** — Hannan City (~1 hour from Namba)
Oldest brewery in Osaka, founded 1716. Tour includes history lecture and possible tank-stirring participation. The building is a designated National Tangible Cultural Property.
- Available April–September only — your May visit is within the window
- Far enough that it's a half-day trip

### Labels to Seek in Osaka

- **Akishika** (Kansai) — 100% estate-grown rice, low-intervention, long-aged, bold high-acidity. The Osaka pride label.
- **Daimon Nigori** — The brewery's flagship unfiltered sake; widely loved
- **Kaze no Mori (Yucho Shuzo, Nara)** — Available throughout Kansai; naturally cloudy, expressive, food-friendly natural sake
- **Nabeshima (Fukuchiyo Shuzo, Saga)** — Rich, umakuchi-style (full, spreading flavor); popular in Kansai bar scene
- **Shichida (Tenzan Shuzo, Saga)** — Rich, velvety, dense; one of the best expressions of the Saga style

## May Seasonality

- **Namazake** (unpasteurized sake) — Peak availability. Spring release season. Ask for "nama" specifically at every bar.
- **Hiyaoroshi** — Fall-release aged sake from previous year's stock; still available at some bars
- The brewing season just ended (fall through spring), meaning toji are sometimes available and some breweries do post-season open days
- Craft Sake Week 2026 was April 17–29 at Roppongi Hills — just before your visit, but the same breweries pour at the bars above year-round
