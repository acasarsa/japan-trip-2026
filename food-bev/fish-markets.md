# Fish Markets: Tokyo, Osaka & Kyoto

Crowd-sourced from Reddit (r/JapanTravel, r/japan, r/tokyo, r/osaka) and travel communities. Compiled May 2026.

For how to order, Japanese phrases, fish kanji, and etiquette: [Sushi Ordering Guide](sushi-ordering-guide.md)

## Tokyo

### The Two Markets: Know the Difference

**Tsukiji Outer Market** is where you eat. The famous inner market (tuna auction) moved to Toyosu in 2018. What remains is ~400 stalls and small restaurants in dense alleyways in Chuo. More tourist-heavy now, but fish quality is still tied to Toyosu wholesale supply — freshness remains excellent.

**Toyosu Market** is the functioning wholesale hub where the tuna auction now happens. You watch from glass observation windows — never on the floor. The restaurant area inside (where Sushi Dai relocated) is excellent, but the market experience itself is sterile and industrial. Consensus: go to Toyosu for specific destination restaurants, but Tsukiji is the better street-eating experience.

---

### Best Spots at Tsukiji

**For Uni (Sea Urchin)**

- **Unitora** — The uni specialist. Miyabi Bowl (¥10,000–¥13,000) lets you taste 5 regional uni sources side-by-side. Emperor Bowl (¥24,500) adds bluefin tuna and ikura. Staff explains each source and welcomes questions. Arrive at 7am on a weekday to walk in without waiting.
- **Sushikuni** — More affordable. "Kobore-don" (overflowing bowl, two types of uni) ~¥3,200–¥3,800. Tip repeated across multiple sources: come after 12:30pm on weekdays and the line often disappears.

**For Tuna**

- **Maguroya Kurogin** — The locally-loved tuna specialist. Deals exclusively in Pacific bluefin from Toyosu. Open 8am–1pm, closed Wednesdays. Star item: **kamatoro** (collar meat behind the gills — only ~500g per 100kg fish, richer than otoro). Watch them slice it at the counter around 8am. Three-Color Sashimi (otoro/chutoro/akami) ¥5,000.
- **Seagen** — Run directly by Yamayuki, one of Japan's most respected maguro wholesale companies (they supply top omakase restaurants). Yamayuki Tuna + Uni + Ikura Bowl ¥2,400 with tuna collagen soup. Ultimate Tuna Bowl (¥4,000) sells out — queue 30 min before opening.

**Hidden Gems**

- **Sushi Okame** — Tiny standing bar, fits 4 people, run solo by the chef-owner. Near Shin-Ohashi and Harumi Dori intersection. Known for **kohada** (gizzard shad) and **shime saba** (cured mackerel) — traditional Edomae fish most tourist stalls skip. Nigiri ¥100–¥500/piece. No menu, point at the blackboard.
- **Aeya** — Run by former Tsukiji inner market tuna auctioneers. Sashimi plates ~¥500, kohada from Tokyo Bay, weekend kaisendon bowls. As close to "market insider" as you can get.
- **Tsukiji Yaki Uo Ishikawa** — Grilled fish specialist (unusual here). Does sashimi-grade fish grilled to order including **nodoguro** (blackthroat sea perch — prized in Japan, unknown abroad).

**What to Avoid**

- **Tamagoyaki on a stick** — Massive lines, Instagram-driven, underwhelming payoff. Don't waste stomach space.
- **Edge-of-market restaurants** on the main roads — tourist premiums. Go deeper into the alleyways for better value.
- **Weekend mornings 10am–noon** — Tsukiji's local neighborhood association has formally asked tour groups to stay away during peak congestion. Go Tuesday–Friday before 9am.

## Osaka

### Osaka Central Wholesale Market

Near JR Noda Station (~10 min walk). The real deal — you're on the floor, not behind glass.

- **Tuna auction at 4:15am**, lasts ~30 min. Taxi required — transit doesn't run early enough. Pre-book through your hotel the night before.
- No tour needed. Multiple visitors report just walking in, finding a corner, and watching without being asked to leave.
- Real working wholesale market: no signs, no English, no info booths. Forklifts, serious professionals. Arrive at 4am.
- By 8am most fish is sold and stalls are closing. Nothing to see after that.

### Endo Sushi

Attached to Osaka Central Market. Open 5am–2pm, closed Sundays. 100+ year history, 4th generation family.

- Omakase set: ~¥1,050–¥1,300 for 5 pieces (always includes toro). Typical: hamachi, toro, tai, uni, anago.
- Chef brushes soy sauce directly on each piece — don't dip.
- Cash only. English menus available.
- Peak crowd 7–8am. Arrive at 5am on a weekday to walk straight in.
- Consistently called the highest value sushi breakfast in Japan by Reddit commenters.

### Kuromon Ichiba Market

Covered market, ~600m long, ~150 shops, 9am–4pm, near Namba. A retail/food street, not wholesale — no auction or floor access. Go to Osaka Central for that. The market association introduced a formal "reasonable pricing system" in 2024 after tourist-gouging complaints.

Best stalls:
- **Futomasa Kuromonichiba** — The locals' fugu specialist. Tessa (paper-thin fugu sashimi) starts ~¥1,000–¥2,000. Tecchiri (fugu hot pot) also available.
- **Shin-Uoei** — 70-year-old shop. Fresh octopus skewers grilled only after you order — the real alternative to tourist-trap takoyaki.
- **Entoki Maguro** — Tuna specialist in business since 1927. Tuna sourced and cut daily.
- **Maguroya Kurogin** — Same brand as Tsukiji; premium bluefin including the rare kamatoro cut.

## Kyoto

Kyoto doesn't have a fish market in the Tsukiji or Osaka sense. Manage expectations here.

### Kyoto Central Wholesale Market

Near intersection of Gojo-dori and Senbon-dori (tell taxi "Kyoto Chuo Oroshi Ichiba"). About 1/5 the size of old Tsukiji.

- Tuna auction at **5am**. Taxi required.
- Access is uncertain — one recent source says public access has been revoked; another visitor reports successfully attending with "a large viewing area upstairs." Verify with the hotel before making the trek.
- No restaurant cluster attached (unlike Endo Sushi in Osaka).
- Sells: maguro, unagi, uni, crabs, and Kyoto's famous kyo-yasai vegetables.

### Nishiki Market

Five-block covered market, 100+ shops. A food shopping and tasting street, not a wholesale market. Best for pickles, Kyoto-style miso, tofu, and pressed sushi.

- **Saba oshizushi** (pressed mackerel sushi) is the Kyoto fish specialty — vinegar-pickled mackerel pressed with sushi rice into a rectangular block. This is what Kyoto did with fish before refrigeration, during the long haul from the coast. Find it at multiple stalls along the market.
- Come before 10am to shop alongside locals. After noon it's elbow-to-elbow.

## What to Order in Late May

Late May is a strong window. Prioritize these — everything here is at or near peak.

| Fish | 日本語 | Notes |
|------|--------|-------|
| Torigai (cockle) | 鳥貝 | Sweet, briny, snappy · **Peak now — gone by June** |
| Katsuo (bonito) | 鰹 | Lean, clean; tataki-style · **Hatsugatsuo season — cultural event, on every blackboard** |
| Aji (horse mackerel) | 鯵 | Oily, rich · Spring peak; good as nigiri or tataki |
| Hotate (scallop) | 帆立 | Sweet, creamy, often raw · Hokkaido peak May–August |
| Shiro ika (cuttlefish) | 白イカ | Sweeter and more tender than squid · Spring–summer peak |
| Murasaki uni | 紫雲丹 | Mild, sweet · Coming into summer peak |
| Bafun uni | 馬糞雲丹 | Richer, more umami · Hokkaido supply still strong |
| Botan ebi (peony shrimp) | 牡丹海老 | Intensely sweet, raw · Ask for the head (fried or salted) |
| Kamatoro | かまとろ | Richer than otoro, melt-in-mouth · Rarest cut; Maguroya Kurogin only |
| Kohada (gizzard shad) | 小鰭 | Briny, acidic, traditional Edomae · Chef's test fish; Okame or Aeya |
| Nodoguro | のどぐろ | Rich, fatty white fish · Prized in Japan; Tsukiji Yaki Uo Ishikawa |

**Skip:** Fugu (October–March peak), oysters (winter peak, quality variable in May).

## Practical Tips

- **Cash is essential** at Endo Sushi, Sushi Okame, most standing bars, and many Kuromon stalls.
- **No tour needed** anywhere on this list. Tours add explanation but cost 3–5x the food price.
- **Days to avoid:** Tsukiji closed Sundays and select Wednesdays. Endo Sushi closed Sundays. Check before going.
- **Don't eat while walking** — considered rude, creates congestion. Use the standing ledge or designated area at each stall.
- **Ordering sequence:** Start lighter (hirame/tai), build to richer cuts (hamachi, chutoro), finish with otoro or uni. Botan ebi in the middle — its sweetness gets lost after strong flavors.
- **English menus:** Available at Endo Sushi, Maguroya Kurogin, Unitora, Sushikuni. Not at Sushi Okame — blackboard only, point and gesture.
