# Translation Tools

External tools that work on the phone — useful when you need something the phrase guide doesn't cover.

| Tool | Best for | Link |
|---|---|---|
| Google Translate | Camera (menus, signs), conversation mode, offline pack | [translate.google.com](https://translate.google.com/?sl=en&tl=ja) |
| DeepL | Higher-quality text translation, more natural phrasing | [deepl.com](https://www.deepl.com/translator#en/ja/) |
| Papago (Naver) | Often better than Google for JP↔EN nuance, Korean too | [papago.naver.com](https://papago.naver.com/?sk=en&tk=ja) |
| Jisho | Japanese-English dictionary, kanji lookup by radical | [jisho.org](https://jisho.org/) |
| Yomiwa | Offline camera kanji recognition (iOS/Android) | [yomiwa.net](https://www.yomiwa.net/) |
| Google Lens | Live camera overlay for signs/menus (in Google app) | built into Google app |

---

## Before you go

**Download Google Translate's Japanese offline pack.** Open the app → Settings → Offline translation → download Japanese. Camera mode still needs data unless you also download the offline image pack.

**Bookmark these on your phone home screen.** In Safari/Chrome: share → "Add to Home Screen." Saves tapping through bookmarks at a restaurant.

---

## When to use which

- **Reading a menu** — Google Lens or Google Translate camera. Point at the kanji, get an overlay.
- **Asking the waiter something specific** — DeepL for translating your English into Japanese, then show the screen. Papago as a second opinion.
- **Looking up a single word** — Jisho. Faster and more accurate than a translator, with stroke order and example sentences.
- **A sign you can't read** — Yomiwa offline if no signal, otherwise Google Lens.
- **Voice conversation** — Google Translate conversation mode (tap the two-mic icon).
