# Sushi Counter Ordering Guide

How to order, what to say, and what not to do at a sushi counter or fish market stall. Covers Tsukiji, Endo Sushi, and casual standing bars.

---

## How Ordering Works

Three modes exist at any sushi counter:

- **Omakase (おまかせ)** — "I leave it to you." Chef selects everything based on the day's catch. Communicate allergies/restrictions before the meal, then receive pieces sequentially. Common at Endo Sushi.
- **Okonomi (お好み)** — À la carte. You choose your own pieces. Order 2–4 at a time, eat them, then order more. Standard at Tsukiji stalls and standing bars.
- **Okimari (お決まり)** — Set menu in tiered grades. Endo Sushi operates this way: choose from 4 preset 5-piece sets.

**The omakase flavor arc** (if you're at a formal counter): white-fleshed fish → silver-skinned fish → shellfish → fatty tuna → uni/ikura gunkan → finishing rolls → tamago (egg signals the meal is ending).

**At Endo Sushi specifically:** warm, loosely-pressed rice; soy sauce is brushed on by the chef — you won't get a separate dipping dish. Cash only. Point to a set or say "omakase de."

For serious allergies, carry a printed Japanese allergy card — verbal communication alone can be insufficient at busy counters.

### Paying and Leaving

| Japanese | Romaji | English |
|----------|--------|---------|
| お会計をお願いします | O-kaikei wo onegai shimasu | Check, please |
| いくらですか？ | Ikura desu ka? | How much is it? |
| 現金のみですか？ | Genkin nomi desu ka? | Cash only? |
| ごちそうさまでした | Gochisousama deshita | Thank you for the meal (always say this when leaving) |
| また来ます | Mata kimasu | I'll come again (a genuine compliment) |

> **Never say "oaiso" (お愛想) to ask for the bill** — it's staff-only language and implies the restaurant has been inhospitable. Always use "o-kaikei onegai shimasu."

## Reading a Blackboard Menu

At market stalls and casual counters, specials are handwritten on a board. A few things to recognize:

| Kanji | Reading | Meaning |
|-------|---------|---------|
| 本日のおすすめ | Honjitsu no osusume | Today's recommendation |
| 旬 | Shun | In season |
| 入荷 | Nyuuka | Just arrived / new today |
| 限定 | Gentei | Limited — today only |
| 鮮魚 | Sengyo | Fresh fish |
| 握り | Nigiri | Nigiri sushi |
| 刺身 | Sashimi | Sashimi |
| 一貫 | Ikkan | One piece |
| 税込 / 税別 | Zeikomi / Zeibetsu | Tax included / Tax not included |

If you can't read it, just ask: **おすすめは何ですか？** The chef will point you in the right direction.

## Etiquette

### Hands or Chopsticks?
Nigiri sushi can be eaten with either — by hand is traditionally correct (it was Edo-period street food). Sashimi is always chopsticks. Read the room at formal counters.

### Soy Sauce
- Dip the **fish side only**, never the rice — the rice absorbs too much and the piece falls apart.
- Use a small amount — the chef has already calibrated the flavor.
- At high-end counters, the chef may apply nikiri (煮切り) directly — if you see a brush stroke on the fish, it's already seasoned. Don't dip.
- Do not mix wasabi into soy sauce at a traditional counter.

### Ginger (Gari)
Eat a small piece **between** different fish to reset your palate. Not a topping — never place it on the sushi. Green tea does the same job.

### Drink Timing
- Sake is traditionally drunk during the otsumami (appetizer) phase, not during nigiri — the rice in sake is said to clash with vinegared shari.
- Beer is fine throughout.
- Leave your glass slightly full to signal you don't want a refill. An empty glass means "please pour more."

### What Not to Do
- Don't bite off half a piece and put the rest back — eat each piece in one bite.
- Don't let sushi sit for more than 30 seconds after it's placed in front of you.
- Don't place your phone or bag on the counter.
- Don't wear strong perfume — it interferes with the aroma of the fish.
- Don't stick chopsticks upright in rice (funeral association).
- Don't walk and eat at market stalls — use the standing ledge provided.
- Don't touch fish on display at market stalls without asking.
- At a standing bar, don't linger after finishing — fast turnover is expected.
